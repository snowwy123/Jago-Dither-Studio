Pattern & texture

Pattern size and angle change ordered checks, dots, hatching and rings. Scatter methods make their own arrangement. Paper grain works with every style.

Place the pattern
Scatter behaviour

A wider reach makes the scatter more open and wiry. Alternating rows reduces directional streaks.

Tone duet

Keep the main style in the shadows and let another pattern appear in the highlights. Lower the meeting point to use more of the second pattern.

Your little tile

Paint the repeating 4 × 4 threshold tile. Low numbers put down ink sooner; high numbers leave more paper. Each square is one pattern cell.

Choose Your little tile as the print style or the second pattern. Save a recipe to share your tile.

Pick your inks

03

Use 2–8 colours. Put your paper colour last. Click an ink to edit its colour or hex value.

Character prints use the darkest ink and lightest paper from your palette.

Colour by tone

Map image brightness to these colours before dithering. While enabled, the map colours become your printing inks. Brightness and Midtones in Print still shape the result.

Keep a good recipe

A recipe saves print, palette and motion settings. Your image stays separate, so you can try it on anything.

Make the ink dance

Works with every print style, including scatter and character art. The marks change while the composition stays in place. It loops independently of whole-image movement.

Bits on the page

Scatter & size variation

More breathing room spreads marks more evenly. Use How many to change the density. Mark size is measured on your page, before layer scaling.

Text stays editable. Fonts come from your device, so lettering can look slightly different on another computer.

The fill belongs to the original composition. Your chosen print inks or gradient map determine the final printed colours.

Send it out into the world.

After-print overlays keep their own colours in image and animation exports. Wobble transfer uses a combined print layer while they are visible, so those colours stay intact. Plain text exports leave them out.

The moving reveal travels across your print and back in GIFs and PNG sequences. Wobble receives the finished print as flattened frames, ready to draw over.

Bring a picture, some words or a moving sketch. Try a print style, mix a few inks, and see where it takes you. The little drawings under Try it are a good place to start.

Make yourself at home

Scroll over the page to zoom. Choose Pan (H) and drag, or use the middle mouse button. Press 0 to fit the page again. In Layers, click a picture or text layer to select it, drag inside to move it, drag a corner to resize, or use the round handle to rotate. Hold Shift for straight moves or 15° turns.

New gives you a fresh page. Change its size later in Layers → The sheet underneath. Layers keep their size; the paper crops what you see without deleting the artwork beyond its edges.

Two studios, one little experiment

Wobble → Dither: export a PNG sequence ZIP from Jago Wobble Studio, then choose Layers → + Wobble loop here. Add printing textures, colour and lettering to the moving drawing.

Dither → Wobble: export a Wobble project and open its .jago file in Wobble. Choose one printed layer or Separate ink layers, then draw over it. Keep your .jagodither original for editing the text and print settings.

Loops can be up to 12 seconds. The return trip supports up to 48 frames; lower the transfer rate for longer loops. The export panel helps you choose.

Keep the good bits

Save keeps the whole project as .jagodither. A recipe keeps settings only. Export PNGs, GIFs, frame sequences, a before/after comparison or a Wobble project. Typewriter club also exports text. There is no automatic project backup, so save anything you want to keep.

Overlays, without drawing a whole new picture

In Layers, choose + Overlay for stars, sparkles, soft lights or dust. Its own settings appear just below the layer name. Use Before dithering for printed texture, or After dithering for clean colour above the print. For a custom drawing, make it in Wobble and bring the loop across.
